01

Our approach

The task is ranking, not classification: given a signal raised on a client, estimate how likely it is to be escalated so analysts can triage. We treated every design choice as a leakage question first — a feature is only valid if it could have been computed from transactions strictly before the signal's own timestamp.

Validation follows a walk-forward scheme (5 folds, 7-day embargo, 365-day purge window) because the real test set is strictly later in time than train — a random split would have quietly leaked the future into the model. Five base learners (LightGBM, HistGradientBoosting, XGBoost, logistic regression) were combined into a weighted_rank ensemble, chosen against three alternatives by nested time-aware meta-CV.

Two extra checks shaped the final pipeline: an adversarial train-vs-test classifier to catch distribution shift, and a synthetic hidden-test audit to sanity-check that our CV score wasn't optimistic.

Dataset overview

3,326 training signals and 832 test signals sit on top of 913,463 raw transactions across 2,498 client keys, spanning 2022-01-02 to 2025-06-29. The median client has 306 transactions of history (mean 366, up to 3102).

Reading the transaction history

The single clearest signal in the data is behavioral, not static: escalated signals are preceded by a sharp ramp in transaction activity, starting roughly 25–30 days out and more than doubling the daily transaction rate by the time the signal fires.

04

Target distribution over time

Monthly escalation rate is noisy (small monthly counts, 3,326 signals over ~21 months) but stays broadly stationary around the 21.6% average across the whole training period — no obvious trend or regime break that would undermine a model trained purely on train-period history. Test-period labels are withheld by design, so this view covers train only.

Cold-start signals (client has little/no prior history) escalate at 23.6% vs 21.5% for warm clients — a real but modest gap. We tested a dedicated cold-start model; it underperformed the main model even on cold rows (0.809 vs 0.917 AUC), so routing was disabled — the general model already generalizes to thin-history clients.

06

How much to trust the score

Because the test period is later than train, we validated with time-respecting folds rather than a random split, and checked the result two more ways before trusting it:

Adversarial validation (can a classifier tell train from test rows?) came back at AUC 0.549 — close to chance, so no material distribution shift between train and test signals.

Calibration is imperfect (ECE 0.049) — the score ranks well but shouldn't be read as a raw probability without recalibration for any hard threshold.

A synthetic hidden-test audit (a held-out slice the pipeline never trains or tunes on) scored 0.8094 AUC against a CV estimate of 0.8548 — a gap worth flagging honestly rather than a clean pass, and the main reason we kept the model simple (rank-ensemble, no heavy stacking) rather than squeezing the last bit of CV score.

07

Conclusion

Three findings drove every modeling decision we made:

  • Behavior before the signal is the strongest lever. A visible acceleration in transaction activity 3–4 weeks pre-signal separates escalated from dismissed cases better than almost any static attribute, and directly motivated our change-point and acceleration feature families.
  • The alert itself already carries signal. signal_type and the bank's own initial_risk_score remain the two single most important model inputs — our engineered features add real but secondary lift on top.
  • Cold-start clients need the same model, not a separate one. Despite escalating slightly more often, thin-history clients were ranked better by the general model than by a dedicated cold-start model, so we kept the pipeline single-model and simpler.

The result is a weighted_rank ensemble scoring 0.8545 OOF AUC / 0.6410 PR-AUC, with an honest audit gap (0.8094 on a held-out synthetic slice) that we'd want to close with more data before treating the score as production-ready rather than a hackathon prototype.
